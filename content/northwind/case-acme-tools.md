# Case study: Acme Tools

## The client

Acme Tools sells hardware. The catalogue runs from hand tools to the sort of equipment a workshop argues about, and the brand had not kept up with the shelves. Online, the company looked like a template: a wordmark set in a default grotesque, photography that changed temperature from category to category, and a colour that existed mostly because a theme had shipped with it.

They came to us for a Brand Sprint in 2025. Varvara Handoga led the work. It was a fixed project, with a beginning and a handover, rather than a monthly queue.

## The problem

Acme's buyers are practical. They notice when a label is sloppy, because they notice when a handle is. The old identity treated the range as generic retail. Nothing in the logo, the colour, or the type suggested that the company knew the difference between a tool you trust and a tool you replace.

Inside the company the gap was just as plain. Sales decks, packaging stickers, and the site header had each invented a slightly different orange. Nobody could say which one was theirs. New product photography arrived and had nowhere consistent to sit.

They needed a system a warehouse supervisor and a frontend developer could both follow, without a brand manager in the room.

## What we did

Varvara Handoga ran the sprint from the object out, not from a moodboard of other retailers. The reference we kept on the wall was the toolbox: enamel, steel, a handle worn where the hand goes, caution colour used sparingly and on purpose. That became the colour system. Neutrals do the catalogue's heavy lifting. One strong accent marks the thing you are meant to find: a primary action, a safety note, the Acme name when it has to hold a busy photograph. We wrote the roles down so the next person cannot invent a second accent because the banner feels quiet.

The logo is a new mark, simple enough to stencil and to set small in a footer. It does not illustrate a specific tool. Acme sells too many, and a saw-blade logo would have dated the moment the range shifted. Type is a pair they license in their own name: one for the name and the headlines, one for the catalogue text, which has to stay readable when a specification runs long.

The brand book collects the decisions. Logo use and clear space, the colour roles, the type, and the rules for photography: real products, real light, no stock handshake standing in for a customer. We included one simple logo animation, the one a Brand Sprint covers: the mark, once, for the site header and for the reel they already play at trade shows. It does not try to be a film.

Varvara walked Acme through the book in a single session and left them with the source files. Packaging and the site were theirs to roll out. We do not print, and we do not build the shop.

## Result

Acme has one mark, one colour system, and a book the team actually opens. The site header, the proposal covers, and the packaging sticker now start from the same file. Trade buyers who knew the old wordmark have a new one to remember, which was the point. The sprint closed with the book. Anything after that — a campaign, a new category, a page that does not fit the templates — is a separate request.
