# Handbook: design critique

Crit is Wednesday at 14:00. The whole session is 45 minutes. It is not 45 minutes per person, and it is not a place to premiere a finished deck you no longer want changed.

Any designer can bring work in progress. Seniors do not have a reserved slot, and juniors are not observing from the wall. If you are holding the file, you can put it on the table. If several people bring something, keep your share short enough that the last person is not presenting to an empty room.

## What you say first

Describe the problem before you offer a solution. The person showing the work states the problem they were given, who it is for, and what they are unsure about. Everyone else does the same before they redraw it out loud.

If you open with a fix ("I'd just make the type bigger"), stop and name the problem you think that fix is for. A solution with no problem is a preference. We have enough of those.

Talk about the work. Not the designer's taste, not their speed, not whether you would have taken the project. Specific frames. Specific states. If you cannot point at it, you are not ready to say it.

## Who it belongs to

You will leave with notes. Notes are not a vote. In the end, the owner decides. The owner is the person whose work it is, not the most senior voice in the room and not the person who spoke last.

If you are the owner, you do not have to adopt the room. You do have to be able to say, afterwards, which notes you took and which you left. A lively Wednesday is not a new brief, and it does not add a revision round. The client still has the two rounds included with the deliverable.

## What does not belong here

New client requests, resourcing, and "can we just quickly look at the invoice". Crit is the work. Pricing and timing go to the account lead, outside the 45 minutes.

Put the file up in Figma before 14:00. Do not spend the first ten minutes finding the page. Explorations are allowed; a blank frame and an apology are not a presentation.
