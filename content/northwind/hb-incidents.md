# Handbook: when something goes wrong

This page is for a client deliverable that is wrong or late. A missed state, a file that went out with the old logo, a first version that will not make the date we gave. It is not a place to build a case against a colleague.

## The day you find out

Tell the account lead the same day. The day you know, not the day you hope to have fixed it, and not the day the client happens to notice. Vladislav Boerdonos and Sandra Tsapljoek are the account leads. If you are the account lead, you already know, and you still write it up.

Same day means that working day. A problem you discover at 17:40 is still that day's message, not tomorrow's awkward opener.

Then write it in Linear, and put the label incident on the issue. The issue says what went out or what missed, which client, and what we are doing next. It does not need a villain. If you cannot write it without a name in the blame slot, you are not ready to file it. Write the facts, then add the label.

## Who tells the client

The account lead tells the client, within one working day. Not the designer, firing off a sorry in Harbor because the silence felt bad. Not a wait to see if the client is angry enough to mention it. One working day, from the account lead, in plain language: what happened, what they will see fixed, and what we need from them if we need anything.

You can be on that message. You do not send a different one of your own.

## How the note should read

The write-up is blameless. Describe the miss and the next step. Leave out character, leave out "X always does this", leave out a theory whose only job is to make someone else the cause. We can talk about how the work is reviewed. We do that after the client has been told, and we do it without a score.

A note that blames a person is the wrong note. Rewrite it. The label is for the work, not a record of someone's character.

## What this page is not

The Harbor outage on 14 April 2026 is not this procedure. File uploads failed for 3 hours 20 minutes, and no files were lost. That outage has its own review. Do not file a portal failure as a design incident, and do not use this page to invent a second story about it.
