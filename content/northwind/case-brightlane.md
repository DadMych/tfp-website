# Case study: Brightlane

## The client

Brightlane makes legal-tech software. Their customers are lawyers and in-house counsel who open a contract and have to decide, clause by clause, what still needs a human. The work is slow on purpose. The software around it had stopped being careful.

They have been on a Studio retainer since 2024. Vladislav Boerdonos is the account lead: he holds the queue, the scope, and the question of whether a request is actually about this product or about a slide someone wants by Thursday. Vlas Koerbatski designed the screens. The retainer suited the job, because the review flow was never going to be settled in a single presentation.

## The problem

The contract review screens had accumulated. A document sat in the centre, which was right, and then everything else competed with it: a comment feed that was not tied to the paragraph you were reading, a toolbar that had gained an action every time a new clause type appeared, filters that mattered only after you already knew what you were looking at.

In usability tests, time to first review was 9 minutes. That is not the legal reading. That is the time before the reading could start: find the file, see what kind of agreement it was, and know which clause was still open. People scrolled. They opened the history because it looked like navigation. They asked a colleague what a highlight was supposed to mean. The contract had the information. The screen spent the first stretch hiding it.

Brightlane did not want a new product. They wanted the first minute of review to feel like the rest of a careful practice.

## What we did

We stayed inside the screens their customers already knew. There was no parallel version to learn, and no new name for the same job.

The document remained the centre. Status moved into the margin, as a small set of labels you can learn once, instead of colour painted through the text of the clause. A long agreement still reads as a document. Comments attach to the clause you have open, so you are not matching a note in a sidebar back to a paragraph number from memory. Actions that are not about the clause in front of you wait until you ask for them.

We drew this in Figma and walked it with Brightlane's product people in short sessions, one list of feedback at a time. Vladislav kept neighbouring ideas — a weekly dashboard, a different empty state for the matter list — in their own requests, so the review flow could finish. Vlas pushed the early frames through the Wednesday crit before they went to the client, mostly to check that the margin labels still worked when a clause ran long.

We did not write their interface into code. The files, the states, and a walkthrough were the delivery. Their own team built it.

## Result

The same style of usability test, run on the redesigned screens, brought time to first review down from 9 minutes to 4 minutes. People reached the open clause and started. We did not claim, and did not measure, a change in how long the legal judgement itself takes. That reading belongs to their customers.

The screens shipped into the product they already sell. Brightlane stayed on the retainer. Later requests have been about the edges of this flow, which is what the first round was for.
