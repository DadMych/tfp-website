# Case study: Quill Notes

## The client

Quill Notes is a note-taking app. The people they care about are students and researchers: long projects, many sources, a tone that should stay calm when the deadline is not. They came to us in 2026 for a Brand Sprint, and they added naming.

Varvara Handoga led the identity. Kwame Mensah made the logo animation. The sprint ran from kickoff on 2 February 2026 to delivery on 2 March 2026.

## The problem

The product had a following and a name people already typed from memory. It did not have an identity that survived contact with a store listing, a slide, or a loading state. The mark was a word in a face the founder had liked in a document. Colour was a single blue, used for everything, including places where it failed as text. Nothing about the brand suggested the way the product actually feels in the hand: quiet, precise, a little severe.

They also wondered whether the name was doing the job. "Quill" sounded like stationery to some of them, and like software to others. They wanted the question answered properly, not in a thread.

## What we did

Naming came first, because a new name would have changed the mark. The add-on produced three name candidates, each with a short rationale: how it sounds when you say it to a supervisor, how it sits next to the product's tone, what it will be mistaken for. We did not file trademarks. The final check, if they had switched, would have been theirs.

They sat with the three. Then they kept its name. Quill stayed Quill. The candidates were useful and then they were done; the decision was that the equity in the existing name was worth more than a cleaner metaphor. Varvara adjusted the identity work the same week, so we did not spend the rest of the sprint decorating a name that had already lost.

The mark is new. It is a wordmark with a small graphic device that can stand alone as an app icon, drawn to stay legible as an app icon. Colour is a restrained set: a paper ground, an ink for text, and one accent that is allowed to mean "this is interactive" and is not asked to carry a second meaning. Type is specified for them to license in their own name. The brand book holds the rules, including what not to do with the mark when a partner asks for a version "with more energy."

Kwame Mensah made the logo animation: a short piece, the one animation a Brand Sprint includes, delivered as the usual motion files. It is the mark arriving, not a film. It runs where the product opens and in the rare moment Quill needs motion somewhere else. He kept it slow. The brief was calm, and a snappy bounce would have argued with the rest of the book.

## Result

Quill launched the new identity under the name it already had. The app icon, the store presence, and the few marketing pages they maintain now come from the same book. They kept its name on purpose, with three alternatives on the table, which is a better reason than habit. The sprint closed on delivery of the book, the mark, and Kwame's animation.
