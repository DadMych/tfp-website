# Case study: Verdant Energy

## The client

Verdant Energy sells energy software to operators who already know their plants and do not need a brand film to explain electricity. What they needed was a marketing site that a technical buyer could stand, and that a non-technical buyer could finish. They commissioned a Website Sprint in 2026, plus an accessibility audit on the designs.

Vlas Koerbatski led the project. Eva de Groot designed with him, and she carried the accessibility pass. We handed the files to Verdant's developers. We did not write the site.

## The problem

The site Verdant had was a single long page that tried to be a brochure, a spec sheet, and a careers poster. Headings were large. The actual claim — what the software watches, and what a human still decides — sat under the fold, in a paragraph the type size had given up on. Diagrams were pictures of diagrams. A keyboard user could not see where they were. Contrast on the green they were proud of failed for body text, which is an awkward position for an energy company to be in.

They wanted fewer ideas, stated properly, on templates their team could fill after we left.

## What we did

We designed 7 templates. Home, product, industries, a customer story, an article, contact, and a landing page for the campaigns their marketing team already runs. There wasn't another page that was truly a different template, and we did not invent one to pad the sprint. A component sheet sits under the templates: navigation, the product diagram as a built component rather than a flat image, quotes, the form, and the states of each.

Eva de Groot ran the accessibility audit add-on across the screens in the sprint. The target was WCAG 2.2 AA. She marked contrast, focus, target size, and anywhere the green was the only carrier of meaning. Findings came back into the templates before handoff, so the audit was not a PDF that contradicted the file. Errors in the contact form are text, not colour alone. Focus is visible on every control we drew. The decorative diagrams have a text equivalent next to them, because a diagram of a grid is not self-explanatory to everyone who needs the page.

Vlas held the structure. Each template has one job. The product page says what the software does. The story page is allowed to be specific. The home page stops trying to be both. Type is large enough for the spec-minded reader and quiet enough that the page does not shout. We designed the breakpoints the templates needed and wrote the notes their developers asked for at handoff, including the token file.

Design QA on a Website Sprint is part of the work. We checked the built pages against the frames before Verdant called the site launched. Eva sat in on that pass so the audit findings were still true in the browser, as far as a visual check can tell. We still do not certify a site. We design to the target, and we say what we checked.

## Result

Verdant has 7 templates, a component sheet, and an accessibility audit that was folded into the file rather than stapled to the end. The marketing team can run a landing page without redrawing the navigation. The green remains; it is no longer asked to be the body text. The sprint ended at handover.
