# Handbook: press, talks and naming clients

Vladislava Wolinska keeps this page. She is head of PR: press and journalist questions, talks and podcasts, the studio blog, publishing case studies, and the client survey.

Invoices, VAT, and contracts are not her job. Invoice questions go to Maksim Ryzjikov. Contract questions go to Vladimir Posjivalov. Do not bounce a journalist to either of them, and do not bounce a contract to her.

## Requests from outside

Any journalist, podcast, or conference request goes to Vladislava the same day. Forward the email as it arrived. Do not draft a reply and send her the draft after you have already answered.

Nobody gives a quote on their own. A short quote counts. A "personal view" counts. A reply you meant to show her afterwards counts. The words that leave the studio are hers, or Oleksij's when she has asked him to speak. If you are unsure the message is press, forward it. A newsletter, a student project, a podcast producer, and a conference organiser are all this page.

Send it on the working day it arrived. A request you see at the end of the afternoon is still that day's forward, not something you park until you have a clever answer.

## Naming a client

Never name a client in public without checking with Vladislava. Public is a talk, a post, the portfolio, and an award entry. A meetup slide is public. A caption under a picture is public. Your personal site is public. A social post is public.

Check before you publish. If she has not answered, you wait. "I cropped the logo" is not a check. "The room was small" is not a check.

## Confidentiality

Clients with a confidentiality clause are never named in public at all. Those clients are Tidepool Payments and Halden Health. Their case studies are internal. No screenshot, no cropped interface, no description careful enough that a reader can still tell who it was. An award entry that makes the product obvious is still naming them.

Do not look for an exception.

## Case studies and talks

Public case studies need the client's approval. Finished work is not approval. A friendly sentence in an old email is not approval unless Vladislava has it as the approval. You can help draft. You do not post the draft while you wait to see if anyone objects.

Talks by staff are booked through Vladislava. If someone asks you to speak, send her the request the same day and leave the slot unaccepted. She decides whether the studio does it, who goes, and what the talk is allowed to show. A talk she booked can still fail the naming rule. You still check before a client name, or a recognisable screen, goes on a slide.
