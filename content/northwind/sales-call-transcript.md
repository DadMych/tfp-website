# Sales call transcript: Nimbus Docs intro call

Intro call, 9 April 2026, about 30 minutes. Sandra Tsapljoek, Northwind Studio, and a prospective client at Nimbus Docs. Nimbus Docs makes document software. Nothing was signed.

[00:00] Sandra Tsapljoek: Hi. Sandra, at Northwind. Thanks for making the time. Can you hear me all right?

Nimbus: Yeah, yeah, you're good. Sorry, I was on mute. Um. So, Nimbus Docs. We make the editor, the sharing, the comments, that whole bit. Document SaaS, basically. We've got a web app and we're, uh, we're trying to work out who should help with the design.

Sandra Tsapljoek: Good place to start. We're a design studio, fourteen people, one office, Lloydkwartier in Rotterdam. Founded in 2017. We do brand, web design, design systems, product design, for software companies. A lot of clients are remote, so the call you're on now is a normal way for us to work.

Nimbus: Rotterdam. OK. We're not in the Netherlands, just so you know. Does that matter?

Sandra Tsapljoek: Not for the work. Meetings sit inside 09:00 to 18:00 CET, weekdays. If a live call is a bad fit we send a Loom instead. We don't do the build.

[04:10] Nimbus: Right, the build. So, um, can I be blunt? Our users keep asking for a mobile app. We don't have one. If we hired you, would the app be part of it? Design and, like, the actual app?

Sandra Tsapljoek: No. We design product screens for the web app. We don't design native mobile apps, and we don't build them. We also don't write production code at all. So an iOS app, an Android app, that isn't us.

Nimbus: That's annoying. Sorry. Not you. We just, everyone we talk to either only does the app or only does the site. Who does the app, then?

Sandra Tsapljoek: Byteharbour. They're a separate company, React Native, based in Amsterdam. The Amsterdam office is theirs. It is not a Northwind office. We have the one office, in Rotterdam. You'd contract Byteharbour directly. We don't subcontract them, we're not responsible for their dates, and there's no referral fee in it for us.

Nimbus: So you just introduce them?

Sandra Tsapljoek: Yes. You contract them directly, their paper, their invoice. If what you actually need built is the website, the web partner we introduce is Pixelforge, in Rotterdam, again not us. Webflow would be Lumen Labs. Same rule. I'm only going there because you asked. Most of this call should be about the design.

[08:40] Nimbus: OK. OK. Um, so what would you actually do for Nimbus Docs? The web app is the product. The marketing site is fine-ish. It's the product UI that's tired. Onboarding, the editor chrome, empty states, that kind of thing.

Sandra Tsapljoek: That's a retainer, not a six-week project. You'd have a monthly pile of design hours. You put requests in Harbor, that's our client portal, and we work through them. Hours are a budget, not a promise that fourteen screens fall out of fourteen hours.

Nimbus: How do people usually buy that? There's more than one size, I assume.

Sandra Tsapljoek: Three. Starter is €2,400 a month, 24 hours, one active request at a time. Studio is the middle. Partner is €11,200 a month, 130 hours, four active requests, a second senior designer, and a monthly review with Oleksij, our studio director. Partner also has an urgent line. Studio does not.

Nimbus: We're not Partner. I can tell you that before you finish the sentence. What's Studio, properly?

[12:15] Sandra Tsapljoek: Studio is €5,800 a month, excluding VAT. 64 design hours. Two active requests at a time. First reply from us the same working day. Figma access is viewer. Editor access is the Partner plan, not Studio. Two revision rounds on a deliverable. A round is one batch of feedback, from one person on your side if you can manage it, and then our update.

Nimbus: €5,800. Say that again so I write it down right.

Sandra Tsapljoek: €5,800 a month. Not including VAT. Dutch clients pay 21%. If you're an EU business outside the Netherlands, it's reverse charge. I don't know which you are yet, so I won't guess on the call.

Nimbus: Don't guess. Our finance person will care and I will get it wrong. Um. What I do need to understand is the hours, because 64 sounds like a lot until it isn't. What if we don't use them?

[16:50] Sandra Tsapljoek: Unused hours roll over one month. They get used first in that next month, and then they expire. They are never refunded. Not at the end of the month, and not if you cancel.

Nimbus: One month. We heard, I don't know, someone said a company got two months. I'm not saying it was you.

Sandra Tsapljoek: One month is the rule. I wouldn't want you planning on anything else. If we ever did something different it would be because we had caused the delay, and it still wouldn't be the policy. I'm not offering that.

Nimbus: Fine. And if we hate it after a quarter?

Sandra Tsapljoek: 30 days' written notice to me. Unused hours still aren't refunded. If you had paid the year up front, unused months come back minus the discount you got on them. The annual discount is 10%, and it doesn't stack with anything else. You don't have to prepay. Monthly is the normal way to start.

Nimbus: We're not prepaying. OK. Different question. We launch in, um, not next week, but soon. Sometimes things catch fire. Can you drop everything?

[21:05] Sandra Tsapljoek: That's a rush, and it's a yes only if I confirm it in writing. Verbally, on a call like this, doesn't count. Rush means we start within one working day and you have a first version within three working days. The fee is 40%. On a retainer we take it in hours, not as a separate invoice. A 10-hour rush uses 14 hours of the 64.

Nimbus: 40%. That's new? I feel like I've seen 25 somewhere.

Sandra Tsapljoek: 40% is what's in force. It changed on 1 March this year. Before that it was 25%. What matters is the date I confirm in writing. A rush I confirmed in February would still be 25% even if we delivered it in March. Anything I confirm now is 40%. And it still depends on capacity. I won't confirm it if the week is full.

Nimbus: Standard, if we don't rush?

Sandra Tsapljoek: We start within three working days, first version within seven. That's the ordinary retainer pace. Working hours are 09:00 to 18:00 CET, Monday to Friday. We're closed on Dutch public holidays and from 24 December through 2 January. A request that lands on a closed day starts the next working day.

[25:30] Nimbus: Who would we actually be talking to? I don't want a different person every week.

Sandra Tsapljoek: Me. I'm the account lead. That's the stable point. Design-wise, Studio is one designer on the work, not a committee. I am not going to name that designer on this call. You haven't signed, I haven't checked the week, and I don't want to promise a person and then move them. You'd meet them at kickoff. Kickoff is within ten working days of signing.

Nimbus: And you, you're not also designing it?

Sandra Tsapljoek: I'm the account lead. I run the requests, the writing-it-down, the "is this in the hours" conversation. The designer owns the file. If you ever moved to Partner, that's when a second senior designer comes on, and when Oleksij joins a monthly review. I'm not pitching that. You already said no.

Nimbus: I did. Um. Can I ask a boring one? Feedback. We are bad at feedback. Five people will write in the Figma file.

Sandra Tsapljoek: You only have two rounds included, so five separate lists would burn them. Put one approver in charge, one list, inside five working days. If we hear nothing for ten working days we treat the round as accepted and we move. Fixing a mistake we made doesn't count as a round. A new page you didn't mention does. That's new work, and it comes out of the hours.

[29:10] Nimbus: I need to take this to the others. We're not signing today. I don't want you to send a contract tonight and have it look like we agreed.

Sandra Tsapljoek: Understood. Nimbus Docs hasn't signed, and this call isn't a yes. I'll send a short recap of Studio at €5,800, the one-month rollover, and the rush rule, so your notes match mine. No invoice, no kickoff, until you tell me in writing that you want to start.

Nimbus: Yeah. That's, um, that's the right size of follow-up. If we do it, the mobile thing is still separate. Byteharbour, not you.

Sandra Tsapljoek: Byteharbour, not us. You contract them directly. I'll put that in the recap in one line so nobody on your side "remembers" that we build apps.

Nimbus: Good. I think that's what I needed. Thanks, Sandra.

Sandra Tsapljoek: Thanks. Talk when you've had the internal conversation. No rush on the reply, and I mean that in the ordinary sense, not the 40% sense.

Nimbus: Ha. OK. Bye.

Sandra Tsapljoek: Bye.
