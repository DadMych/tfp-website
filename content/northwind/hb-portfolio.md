# Handbook: portfolio and confidentiality

*Internal. Last updated 28 October 2025. Read this before you put client work on the site, in a deck, or in a talk.*

We may show finished work after it has launched. Not before. Drafts, rejected routes, and anything still in review stay inside the project. The client owns the final files once they are paid for. The right to show the work is separate from that, and it is not automatic if the contract says otherwise.

## Check Notion first

Before you post, open the client's page in Notion. Look for a confidentiality clause. Your memory is not the record. People sign, and then six months later someone crops a screenshot into a slide.

- No clause on the Notion page: portfolio use is allowed after launch.
- A clause is on the page: do not post. That includes the website, pitch decks, meetup slides, and a Loom you were about to send outside the studio.
- The clause is free at signing. If a new client wants one, say yes and write it on the Notion page the day the agreement is signed. Don't leave it in a chat thread.

If the page is empty or you're not sure the note is current, ask the account lead before you publish. Guessing wrong is the expensive version.

## Tidepool and Halden

Tidepool Payments and Halden Health have a confidentiality clause.

For both of them:

- No public case study images. No cropped UI in a deck, no "anonymous" screenshot that is obviously their product, no animation still.
- A written case study is allowed only once it has been approved. Approval lives on the Notion page. If you can't find it, it isn't approved, and you don't draft one in public "to see how it feels".

Don't describe the work in enough detail that the client is identifiable, and don't assume a private Slack export is private enough to paste into a talk.

## Everything else

Other clients can be shown after launch, unless their Notion page says otherwise. Check the page anyway. A clause can be added at signing on a later project, and the old case study you remember is not a clearance.

When you do post:

- Use launched work, not an exploration frame.
- Don't include their unreleased product copy if the launch only covered the brand.
- Credit the people who did the work the way the account lead has it on the Notion page. Don't invent a team.

If a client asks us to take something down, tell the account lead the same day and take it down. Don't argue about the clause in the comments of the post.
