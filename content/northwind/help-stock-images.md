# Stock photos and illustrations

Pictures in a mockup are there to test the layout: crop, contrast, how type sits on a photograph, whether an illustration carries the page. They are not a green light to ship that image.

## Treat the mockup as a placeholder

Stock images used in our mockups are a placeholder. The photo, the illustration, the texture behind a headline: assume it is standing in until you hold a licence for the real use. A crop that looks finished in a Figma frame is still a placeholder if nobody has bought the rights for production.

You buy that licence. We do not buy it for you, and we do not transfer one we happen to have used while designing. Production use is your purchase, for the medium you are actually publishing: the site, the deck, the campaign page.

If a launch is close and the images are still the ones from the mockup, stop and check the licence before you publish. Replacing a placeholder late is cheaper than publishing someone else's picture.

## If you want us to propose options

We can propose images. Say so in the Harbor request, and say where they will be used. A proposal is a shortlist and a point of view. It is not a purchase, and it is not approval to use the file we dropped into the frame.

When you have bought the licence, tell us, and we will place the final image. Until that message, the frame stays a placeholder, even if everyone internally has started calling it final.

## Generated images

AI-generated imagery is not the default, and it is not a quiet substitute for stock. We use it only with your approval, given before it goes into the work. When we do use it, it is labelled as AI-generated, in the file and in the note that comes with the delivery.

We will not ship an unlabelled generated image because it filled a hole in a layout. If you have not approved that kind of image, you will not find one in the handoff.
