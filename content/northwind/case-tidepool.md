# Case study: Tidepool Payments

Internal write-up. Tidepool Payments has a confidentiality clause. No screens, no flow diagrams, and nothing from this page goes on the public site. Text cleared with their legal team before it was filed.

## The client

Tidepool Payments is a fintech. The product moves money, and the first thing a new customer does is prove they are allowed to. That proof is the onboarding flow: identity, business, the questions a compliance team cannot skip and a customer will abandon if the screen is careless.

They are on a Partner retainer. Sandra Tsapljoek runs the account. Tomás Pereira designed the flow. Oleksij van Koeropatkin joins the monthly review, as he does for every Partner client.

## The problem

Onboarding was where Tidepool lost people, and the loss was not a mystery to their compliance team. The steps were legally necessary. The screens were not. Questions arrived in the order the policy had been written, which is not the order a person can answer them. Help text quoted the internal name of a check. Errors said that something had failed and not what the customer was supposed to do on a Tuesday afternoon.

Drop-off in onboarding sat at 42%. Tidepool measured that. A large share of the people who left were not rejected. They stopped. Support could see the step. They could not fix the step by rewriting a tooltip in the product admin, because the confusion was the structure.

Their legal team had to stay in the work. A plainer sentence that is wrong is worse than a stiff sentence that is right. We were not going to "make it friendly" and hope counsel caught up.

## What we did

Tomás redesigned the flow as a series of screens with one decision each. The order follows what the customer can know at that moment: who you are, then the business, then the documents, then the review. Where a step exists only because a rule requires it, the screen says so in language counsel signed, and it says what happens after the customer provides it.

Every string that touched a requirement went through Tidepool's legal team. We drafted in the file, they marked up a list, and we did not ship a friendlier synonym that they had not seen. Sandra kept that loop inside the retainer so it did not become a side project with its own tone of voice. The monthly review with Oleksij was where we checked that the flow was still the priority when a campaign page tried to jump the queue.

States are designed, not implied: the document that is still uploading, the check that is pending and is not a rejection, the error that names the field, the confirmation that tells the customer whether they can use the product today or whether a person at Tidepool still has to look. Empty states do not cheer. This is not that kind of product.

We designed. Tidepool's engineers built. Copy that counsel had approved was in the handoff file as the copy, not as a suggestion next to lorem ipsum.

## Result

Drop-off in onboarding fell from 42% to 27%. The figure is Tidepool's, from the same point in the flow they had been watching. We do not dress it up further. Some of the people who still leave are failing a check, and that is the product working. Fewer people leave because they cannot tell what the screen wants.

The retainer continues. Later work stays off this page. The confidentiality clause covers the flow, the copy, and the numbers behind the two we were allowed to write down.
