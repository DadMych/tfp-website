# Podcast interview: Oleksij van Koeropatkin

Recorded 2 June 2026. Host and Oleksij van Koeropatkin. Some crosstalk left in.

[00:00] Host: Welcome back. Today I've got Oleksij van Koeropatkin, who founded Northwind Studio. Oleksij, hello. For people who don't know the studio, what do you actually do?

[00:18] Oleksij van Koeropatkin: Hello. Um, we're a design studio in Rotterdam. One office, in Lloydkwartier. We work for software companies: brand identity, web design, design systems, product design. A lot of the clients are somewhere else. Europe, North America. The studio stays here.

[00:52] Host: And you started it.

[00:55] Oleksij van Koeropatkin: I founded it in 2017. So, 2017, Rotterdam, me deciding I didn't want to be the design department inside someone else's company. That's the whole origin story. It's not more dramatic than that.

[01:30] Host: A lot of studios your age have either stayed tiny or become a much bigger company with a second office. You haven't. How big are you?

[01:48] Oleksij van Koeropatkin: We're 16 people. I get asked when we're going to "properly" grow, and the answer is I'm not planning to. I want to stay at 16 people. At this size I still know which project is stuck, and the account leads still know the work, not just the account. Past that, in my experience of watching other studios, you add a layer whose job is to explain the work to you. I don't want that job to exist.

[02:55] Host: Sixteen including you?

[03:00] Oleksij van Koeropatkin: Yes. 16 people, me included. Designers, two account leads, operations and finance, two lawyers, a head of PR, me. I'm not extra.

[03:20] Host: You don't build the products, though. I've heard that and I want to make sure I have it right, because a lot of listeners will assume you do the front end.

[03:35] Oleksij van Koeropatkin: We don't. We don't write production code. We don't build mobile apps. We don't run ads, we don't manage social media, and we don't do video beyond a simple logo animation. If a listener only remembers one thing, remember that. It saves everyone a proposal.

[04:15] Host: That feels like leaving money on the table.

[04:22] Oleksij van Koeropatkin: It's leaving a different company on the table. If we hired engineers we'd have to run engineering. The work would get worse, not bigger, because the critique would split. I care about the design being right. Someone else should care about the build being right. Those can be friends. They shouldn't be the same standup.

[05:10] Host: So when a client says "and can you ship it?"

[05:16] Oleksij van Koeropatkin: We introduce them to a partner, and then we step out of the contract. Pixelforge does web, they're in Rotterdam too, a neighbour, not our team. Byteharbour does React Native, mobile apps, and their office is in Amsterdam. That office is theirs. It is not a Northwind office. We have one office. Lumen Labs does Webflow. The client contracts them directly. We don't take a referral fee. If you hire them, you hire them. We're not in the middle of that invoice.

[06:30] Host: Ok. Different complaint I hear about studios. Revisions that never end. You've got a hard line on this, haven't you?

[06:45] Oleksij van Koeropatkin: Two rounds. Two rounds included, per deliverable. That's the policy.

[06:58] Host: Clients must hate that on day one.

[07:05] Oleksij van Koeropatkin: They hate the third "quick pass" more, once they've lived through it. Before 1 January 2025 we included three rounds. What actually happened was feedback arrived in pieces. A note on Monday, a contradictory note on Thursday, a founder who hadn't looked yet. The work got blurry. We cut it from three rounds to two so a round would mean one consolidated response. Better work, shorter projects, fewer people pretending a fourth opinion is a new requirement.

[08:20] Host: And if they miss the window?

[08:25] Oleksij van Koeropatkin: Feedback is due within five working days. If we hear nothing for ten working days, the round is accepted. Silence is an answer. It has to be, or a project sits open until someone's holiday ends and then it's somehow our delay.

[09:00] Host: What if they genuinely need another round?

[09:08] Oleksij van Koeropatkin: Then it's an extra round, and it's priced. On a fixed-price project an extra round is €450. On a retainer it comes out of the hours. We don't do a free one "because the relationship is good". The relationship stays better if the round means something.

[09:45] Host: Hours. You've got retainers. Talk about the big one, because I think it's new.

[09:55] Oleksij van Koeropatkin: Partner. We introduced it on 1 January 2026. There wasn't a Partner plan before that. It's €11,200 a month, before VAT, 130 hours, four active requests. A second senior designer. And a monthly review with me, not as a sales call, as a review of the work. There's an urgent line on Partner as well. Working hours, plus 07:00 to 09:00 CET. It is not on the smaller plans. We talked about extending it and decided it stays Partner-only.

[11:15] Host: And the smaller ones, so people have the scale?

[11:20] Oleksij van Koeropatkin: Starter is €2,400 a month, 24 hours, one active request. Studio is €5,800, 64 hours, two active requests. Same rule on all of them: the hours are a budget, not a promise of a fixed pile of screens. Some weeks a hard problem eats the hours. That's the work. Pretending otherwise is how studios end up showing fake velocity.

[12:20] Host: You said you review Partner work yourself. With 16 people, is that actually possible?

[12:32] Oleksij van Koeropatkin: That's the reason for the cap. A monthly review with a handful of partners I can do properly. A monthly review with many more would turn into a status meeting, and I already know what those are worth. Staying at 16 people is what makes the Partner review real. If the plan only works by hiring a layer between me and the work, the plan is wrong, not the headcount.

[12:48] Host: Can I ask about the people who aren't designing? You've got two lawyers and a head of PR. Why does a design studio keep them?

[13:02] Oleksij van Koeropatkin: They don't design, and that's the point. A contract question and a journalist are not a critique. Vladimir Posjivalov handles the master services agreement, statements of work, contract changes, confidentiality, non-solicitation, IP. Sofie Milkova handles data protection, the DPA, file deletion requests, NDAs. Two different kinds of careful. I don't want them folded into one person, and I don't want a designer doing either between screens.

[13:20] Host: And the head of PR?

[13:24] Oleksij van Koeropatkin: Vladislava Wolinska. Press, talks, podcasts, the blog, publishing case studies, the client survey. Nobody talks to a journalist or names a client in public without her. If a client has a confidentiality clause, we don't name them at all. I'd rather have one person whose job that is than have it be whoever answered.

[13:30] Host: Remote clients, Rotterdam hours. How does that not become you on calls until midnight?

[13:42] Oleksij van Koeropatkin: We work 09:00 to 18:00 CET, Monday to Friday. We don't do the midnight call. For a client who can't make a live review we record a Loom and they watch it on their morning. Harbor is where the request lives, so we're not reconstructing the brief from three inboxes. It is less glamorous than a follow-the-sun studio. It is also how the work stays good on a Friday.

[14:40] Host: Anything you wish a client would ask on the first call?

[14:48] Oleksij van Koeropatkin: Ask what we won't do. Code, mobile apps, ads, social, video past a logo animation. And ask how many revision rounds, and what happens to unused hours. They roll over one month, then they expire, and they are never refunded. People hear "hours" and think "bank account". It isn't.

[15:40] Host: Oleksij van Koeropatkin, Northwind Studio, founded in 2017, still 16 people, still not writing your code. Thank you.

[15:52] Oleksij van Koeropatkin: Thank you. That was, um, that was the tidy version. The untidy version is the same facts, just with more coffee.

[16:05] Host: We'll take the tidy version. Thanks for coming on.
